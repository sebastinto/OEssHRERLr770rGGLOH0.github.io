    QUESTIONS

    Questions worth answering

    How do I get my music onto the iPhone?
    Three ways. Open the Wi-Fi transfer screen and drag a folder from your Mac in the browser, AirDrop an album straight into K7, or pick any folder already sitting in Files. Tags, artwork and folder structure are read on the way in.
    Which formats does it play?
    FLAC, ALAC, MP3, AAC, M4A, WAV, AIFF, OGG, Opus, DSF, DFF, MPC, TTA and WavPack — fourteen in all, up to 24-bit / 192 kHz output. WMA, APE and AC3 are not included, for patent and licensing reasons rather than technical ones. Broken or partially tagged files still play; K7 falls back to the filename. Files with old FairPlay DRM are recognised and explained rather than silently skipped.
    Does it touch my Apple Music library?
    No. K7 has no access to it and never asks for permission to read it. K7 plays only the files you import, and it never modifies, uploads or re-encodes anything outside the folders you point it at.
    Is there a subscription?
    No. There is no recurring charge and no server bill to pass on. One purchase buys K7 and every update to it, on every device on your Apple ID. If a future version is a substantial rework rather than an update, it may ship as a separate paid app with a discounted upgrade path for existing owners.
    What about the car, widgets and the lock screen?
    Lock screen artwork, Live Activities, a home screen widget, Shortcuts actions and Siri playback are all included, and playback controls appear on your car's screen through CarPlay's Now Playing. Browsing your library from the car is not in the first release — it needs an entitlement Apple only grants to apps already on the store, so it is the first thing on the list after launch.
    What leaves my phone?
    Nothing, unless you ask for it. K7 has no account, no analytics and no tracking of any kind. Artwork search sends the album and artist to the music catalogues and nothing else. Studio is off until you add your own Anthropic, OpenAI or Google key, and then your prompt and a summary of your library go from your phone straight to that provider, billed to you — never any audio, never a file path. Each is asked for separately, and declining leaves the rest of the app working.
