### **Information Collection and Use**

K7 has no account, no sign-in and no server of its own. We do not collect, receive or store any personal information about you. Your music, your tags, your playlists, your settings and your listening history stay on your device.

K7 makes no network request at all unless you ask it to, and every kind of request is described below. Each one is shown to you in full, and asked for separately, before the first time it is made. Declining leaves the rest of the app working.

### **Your Music Stays on Your Device**

K7 reads the audio files you import and the tags and artwork inside them. Nothing about your library is uploaded, copied to us, re-encoded or shared. We never see your files, your file names, your folder structure or what you listen to. K7 does not scan your device beyond the folders you point it at.

K7 does not request access to your Apple Music library and cannot read it.

### **Album Artwork Lookups**

If you search for cover art, K7 sends the album and artist you are searching for to the music catalogues, and nothing else — not your library, not your files, not who you are. The catalogues it may contact are musicbrainz.org, coverartarchive.org, api.deezer.com and itunes.apple.com.

When you choose a cover, K7 downloads that image from wherever the catalogue points, which is usually not the catalogue's own domain. K7 identifies itself to these services by name and by a link to its web page, because MusicBrainz requires a contactable client. It sends no identifier for you or your device.

### **Web Search for Covers**

If you use the web search for a cover that the catalogues do not have, K7 sends the album and artist as a search phrase to [Serper](https://serper.dev/), together with the search key you supplied, so the search is billed to your own account. It sends nothing else — not your library, not your files, not who you are. Declining leaves the catalogues working; only the web search stops.

### **Studio Playlists**

Studio is optional and off until you add your own API key. If you use it, K7 sends your prompt together with a summary of your library — titles, artists and albums, tags and play counts, and the names of the folders you imported — to the model provider you chose: [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/), [OpenAI](https://openai.com/) or [Google](https://ai.google.dev/). It never sends audio, never a file path, and never your name or your device.

The request goes from your phone straight to your provider, billed to your own key. It does not pass through us, and we never see your prompt or your library. What your provider does with it is governed by their privacy policy, linked above. Declining leaves everything else in K7 working exactly as it does now.

### **Your API Keys**

The keys you add for Studio and for web search are stored in the iOS Keychain on your device. They are used only to authorise requests you have asked for, are never sent anywhere except to the provider they belong to, and are never sent to us.

### **Wi-Fi Transfer**

Wi-Fi transfer opens a page on your local network so you can drag music from a computer straight to your device. It runs only while that screen is open and closes when you leave it. The connection stays on your own network; nothing is uploaded to us or to anyone else, and no data leaves your network. Anyone on the same network who has the address can reach that page while it is open, so use it on a network you trust.

### **Analytics and Tracking**

K7 contains no advertising, no attribution or measurement code, and no third-party tracking SDK. It does not track you across other apps or websites, and it does not build a profile of you.

K7 includes no analytics. It does use crash and stability reporting, which is described below.

### **Crash and Stability Reporting**

To find and fix the failures we would otherwise never hear about, K7 uses [Sentry](https://sentry.io/). When the app crashes or hangs, Sentry receives the error, the app version, and your device model and operating system version.

It is deliberately narrower than Sentry's defaults. K7 sends no screenshot, no view hierarchy, no session replay, no performance traces, no personal information, and no identifier for you or your device. It records no automatic breadcrumbs, including none for network requests — those would carry the album titles you searched for.

Nothing about your library reaches it: not a track title, not a file path, not a folder name. This is the only monitoring in the app.

You can turn it off. Settings has a Crash reporting switch under Diagnostics; switching it off stops the reporter immediately rather than at the next launch, and nothing further is sent. There is no advertising, no behavioural analytics, and no tracking of you across other apps or websites, and the reports are not used to identify you.
